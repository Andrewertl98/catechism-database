QUR'ANIC REFERENCES USED IN THE APOLOGETICS TOPIC — MUST BE VERIFIED BEFORE PUBLISHING
Created Aug 21, 2026.

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
Five surah references appear in apologetics question prompts. Every Catholic
citation in this database was checked against a real text; these were not, and
they cannot be, by the same method -- there is no Qur'anic corpus in this
repository to check them against, and translations differ in wording even where
they agree in substance.

They are recorded here so verification is a specific task with a checklist,
rather than a vague intention.

REFERENCES TO VERIFY
  Surah 5:47   (Al-Ma'idah)   People of the Gospel to judge by what was revealed in it
  Surah 5:68   (Al-Ma'idah)   People of the Scripture stand on nothing until they uphold
                              the Torah, the Gospel, and what was revealed to them
  Surah 10:94  (Yunus)        If in doubt, ask those who have been reading the Book before you
  Surah 3:3-4  (Ali 'Imran)   Allah sent down the Torah and the Gospel before, as guidance
  Surah 18:27  (Al-Kahf)      None can change the words of Allah

  Used in: apologetics-l6-007, apologetics-l6-010

FOR EACH ONE, CONFIRM
  1. The surah and verse numbers are correct.
  2. The substance is fairly represented in a mainstream translation, and the
     question does not lean on a rendering that is contested.
  3. The verse is not being read against its context in a way a Muslim reader
     would recognise as unfair. This is the one that matters most: an argument
     that only works on a strained reading is worse than no argument.

A CORRECTION ALREADY MADE
The "multiple Qur'ans gathered up" argument was NOT included. That account --
variant codices collected and burned under Uthman -- comes from hadith
(Bukhari), not from the Qur'an. Since the brief was to argue from the Qur'an
alone, including it would have misattributed a hadith to the Qur'an, which is
exactly the error that lets an informed reader dismiss the whole module. If that
argument is wanted, it needs to be framed openly as drawing on Islamic
tradition rather than Qur'anic text.
